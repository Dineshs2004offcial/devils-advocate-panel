# Revenue Intelligence Market Report

## Category Definition
Revenue intelligence platforms differ from sales intelligence (which helps identify opportunities via contact/company data) by converting existing pipeline into predictable revenue — through call/conversation analysis, deal-risk scoring, forecasting, and GTM alignment tooling. Gong is widely regarded as the category leader; Clari and ZoomInfo's Chorus are the other major named players. As of December 2025, Gartner formally merged "sales engagement" and "revenue intelligence" into a single Revenue Action Orchestration (RAO) Magic Quadrant category (see the Sales Engagement Market Report for detail).

## 2026 Trend Themes
Industry coverage (Rox.com) identifies six trends shaping revenue intelligence in 2026:
1. Agentic revenue workflows — AI agents automating routine tasks and providing contextual recommendations
2. Revenue signal orchestration — aggregating buying signals across systems
3. AI-powered sales automation moving from task automation to strategic execution
4. Workflow intelligence and execution visibility investment
5. Context-aware revenue systems reducing tool-switching
6. (implied) deeper CRM-native integration to avoid a separate system of record

## Pricing Reality (Gong as Category Bellwether)
Gong pricing is notably non-transparent and layered rather than a single per-seat number: per-user license fees of roughly $1,200-$2,500+/year depending on tier, PLUS a mandatory platform fee of $5,000-$50,000/year, PLUS $7,500-$28,500 in one-time implementation costs. A 10-person team's realistic Year 1 total lands around $21,000-$29,000, with multi-year contracts carrying 5-15% automatic annual price uplifts. This "three cost layer" pricing structure (per-seat + platform + implementation) appears to be characteristic of the revenue-intelligence sub-category more broadly, distinguishing it from simpler per-seat sales engagement tools.

## Competitive Dynamic
Gong's stated position is "Revenue AI Operating System" — capturing calls, emails, and meetings and analyzing them for risk/coaching signals. Its acknowledged limitation (per third-party reviews) is that it analyzes calls *after* they happen rather than helping generate the pipeline or in-market signals that lead to those calls — a gap that upstream sales-intelligence/ABM tools (ZoomInfo, 6sense, Clay) are positioned to fill.
