# SaaS Pricing Models (2026)

## The Structural Shift Away From Per-Seat
Per-seat/per-user pricing — the default SaaS model for over a decade — is in structural decline. It still accounts for roughly 58% of SaaS products, but 42% of products now offer a usage-based/consumption pricing option, up from 27% in 2023. The shift began during the 2022-2024 correction, when buyers began negotiating hard on unused seats, and accelerated in 2025-2026 as AI-native tools introduced consumption patterns that flat per-seat billing cannot capture (an AI feature used heavily by 10% of seats and barely at all by the other 90% breaks the per-seat pricing logic).

## Usage-Based / Credit-Based Pricing
85% of SaaS companies surveyed (Metronome/Greyhound Capital, Jan 2025, n=100) already have some usage-based pricing live. A specific emerging pattern is "credit-based pricing" — customers purchase a bucket of credits upfront and consume them per action (a survey question, a report generated, a query resolved), functioning as an internal currency tied to value delivered. Credit wallets are becoming a standard supporting mechanism for this model.

## AI Surcharges
73% of vendors now charge separately for AI features as of 2025-2026 — making AI monetization the fastest-moving pricing variable in the current market.

## Counter-Trend: Pushback Toward Predictable Pricing
Notably, Salesforce's Marc Benioff has publicly pushed back against per-conversation/credit-based AI pricing after customer feedback, stating customers "pushed for more flexibility" and advocating a return to predictable per-user pricing for Agentforce — indicating the market has not fully converged on usage-based/credit pricing as the clear winner, and buyer preference for pricing predictability remains a live tension.

## Median Pricing Increases
Median SaaS vendor price increases run around 7.8% YoY (2026 data), with 79% of IT leaders reporting they faced a price increase at their last renewal.

## Model Selection Guidance by Stage/Product Type
- Usage-based fits products with clear, variable-value units of consumption (API calls, records processed, AI-generated outputs).
- Per-seat remains simplest for products with roughly even usage across seats and where buyer budget planning favors predictability.
- Credit-based/hybrid models are increasingly used to bridge both: base subscription (predictable) plus consumption credits (usage-aligned) for AI or variable-cost features specifically.

## Framing for a Fundraising Deck
A pricing model choice should be explicitly justified against this 2026 market context — a pure per-seat model for an AI-heavy product may read as under-monetizing relative to current market norms, while pure usage-based pricing for a predictable-workflow product may create buyer budget-planning friction that per-seat avoids.

Sources: Stripo Research "SaaS Pricing Trends 2026" (citing Zylo SaaS Management Index, OpenView pricing survey, Metronome/Greyhound Capital survey, CloudNuro); Ibbaka/Pricing Innovation "Pricing Predictions for 2026" (Benioff quote and credit-pricing pushback).
