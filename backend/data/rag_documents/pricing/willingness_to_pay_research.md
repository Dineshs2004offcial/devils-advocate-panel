# B2B SaaS Willingness-to-Pay (WTP) Research

## The Core Finding: Most Companies Don't Measure This
Only 28% of SaaS companies regularly conduct willingness-to-pay research with customers and prospects, per OpenView Partners' 2023 SaaS Benchmarks report — the remaining 72% are effectively setting prices without direct evidence of what the market would actually bear.

## Why It Matters Financially
Per Price Intelligently research, a 1% improvement in pricing strategy yields an average 11.1% increase in operating profit — significantly more impactful than a comparable 1% improvement in customer acquisition cost (3.2% profit impact) or retention (6.7% profit impact). Pricing is the highest-leverage lever available to a SaaS business, yet it is the least frequently and rigorously tested.

## The "High WTP, Wrong Packaging" Pattern
A recurring finding across multiple 2025-2026 sources: B2B markets often *do* have high willingness to pay for solutions that deliver clear business outcomes/ROI — the failure mode is not an absence of budget, but that vendors package and price in ways that don't capture the value the market would actually pay for. Feature-level pricing research (surveying customers about individual features, running conjoint analyses, A/B testing small packaging tweaks) is specifically flagged as a common but flawed methodology that misses this dynamic, because it optimizes packaging at the margin rather than testing whether the core value proposition supports a higher price point entirely.

## Buyer Maturity as a WTP Driver
How much a prospect is willing to pay is influenced substantially by "buyer maturity" — how evolved the prospect is in understanding their own problem, the available solution landscape, and the value those solutions provide. Less sophisticated buyers anchor on price; more sophisticated buyers anchor on quantified outcomes, meaning the same product can command different WTP depending on how commercially mature the buying committee is.

## 2026 Macro Pricing Shift Relevant to WTP
85% of surveyed SaaS companies (Metronome/Greyhound Capital, Jan 2025 survey of 100 companies) already have usage-based pricing in place, and 73% of vendors now charge separately for AI features — meaning WTP research increasingly needs to be conducted per pricing dimension (seats, usage, AI features) rather than as a single blended "what would you pay" question, since buyers may have very different WTP thresholds for each component.
