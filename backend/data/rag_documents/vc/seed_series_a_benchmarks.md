# Seed & Series A Fundraising Benchmarks (2026)

## Valuation Benchmarks (Carta, Q4 2025 data)
- Pre-seed: median post-money valuation ~$5.87M globally (Equidam Q1 2026 dataset, 3,000+ pre-seed valuations), with a median capital requirement of ~$0.67M; US valuations run 20-40% higher than comparable startups in other regions.
- Seed: median post-money valuation reached $24M in Q4 2025, up from $18M one year earlier and $16M two years earlier — a rapid two-year escalation.
- Series A: median post-money valuation reached $78.7M in Q4 2025, up 37% YoY from $57.5M.
A separate, slightly more conservative 2025 estimate cites median seed pre-money at $10-15M and median Series A pre-money at $30-50M — the discrepancy versus Carta's post-money figures partly reflects post- vs. pre-money framing and dataset differences; always confirm which convention a given benchmark uses.

## Series A Bar (2026 specifics, WaveUp "Rules of Survival")
- B2B SaaS median ARR at Series A: $3M (up sharply from $1M in 2021) — the ARR bar for a Series A raise has roughly tripled in five years.
- Burn multiple: under 1.0x-2.0x expected (see Finance/burn-rate document for full framework)
- NRR: 120%+ expected
- LTV:CAC: 3-5x+ expected
- Runway target: 24 months is "the new 18," with some VCs pushing for 36 months
- 80% of financial models reviewed by VCs reportedly fail to clearly show the "cash gap" between current runway and the next raise — a specific, correctable diligence weak point for founders to address proactively.

## Series A "Default Alive" Filter
Paul Graham's 2015 "default alive" framework (a company that will reach profitability before running out of cash if expenses stay flat) has become the primary Series A investor filter in 2026, operationalized as: burn multiple under 2x required just to get a meeting. The current Series A benchmark is described as $1.5-2M ARR with 100% YoY growth as a rough floor for institutional-quality traction, though the $3M median cited above suggests the bar in practice often runs higher than this "floor."

## AI Funding Concentration
Crunchbase reported $300 billion in global venture funding in Q1 2026, with approximately 80% going to AI companies — meaning non-AI-native B2B SaaS companies are competing for a shrinking share of available capital and should expect closer scrutiny of their AI positioning and roadmap as a result.
