# Startup Valuation Methods

## Method 1: Comparable Companies (Comps) — Most Common at Growth Stage
Identify 5-10 public or recently-funded comparable companies, pull their key metrics (revenue/ARR, EBITDA, gross profit), calculate market-cap-to-metric multiples, and apply those multiples to the target company's own metrics. Typical 2025-2026 multiple ranges by sector: SaaS 8-15x ARR (higher for high-growth, lower for slow-growth); fintech 3-8x revenue; consumer 2-5x revenue; marketplace 4-10x gross profit. A private-company discount of 20-30% is typically applied versus public comps to reflect illiquidity. High-growth SaaS at seed/Series A can command 10-30x ARR in a strong market, per some 2025 sources — a wide enough range that citing a specific multiple requires specifying growth rate and stage.

## Method 2: Precedent Transactions
Uses actual M&A/financing deal multiples from comparable recent transactions rather than public-market comps. Limitation: transaction terms are often undisclosed or incomplete, and multiples from a prior market cycle (e.g., 2021's "frothy" multiples) no longer apply and should be excluded from a current analysis.

## Method 3: Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)
Projects free cash flows 5-10 years forward, applies a risk-adjusted discount rate (typically 15-40% for startups), and discounts back to present value including a terminal value. Widely regarded as theoretically "correct" but the most dangerous method for early-stage startups because the output is entirely dependent on the accuracy of long-range projections that are inherently speculative pre-revenue or pre-scale.

## Method 4: The VC Method
Combines DCF and relative valuation: (1) select a terminal/exit year (7-10+ years for seed, compressing to 1-3 years for bridge/mezzanine stage), (2) estimate exit value by applying a comparable multiple to projected success-scenario financials, (3) discount to present value using a high hurdle rate (30-70%+) that compensates for risk and founder/investor optimism bias, (4) back into the required ownership percentage and adjust for expected future dilution across subsequent rounds.

## Method 5: Scorecard/Berkus Method (Pre-Revenue Only)
For pre-revenue seed/pre-seed companies where no financial metrics exist yet, investors rate team, market size, product, and traction qualitatively against comparable deals, then apply a rule-of-thumb pre-money range (commonly cited: pre-seed $3-8M, seed $8-20M pre-money, though see the Seed/Series A Benchmarks document for more current 2025-2026 figures which run higher).

## Practical Guidance
Pre-revenue valuation leans on Scorecard/Berkus/VC methods (valuing team, market, and vision); post-revenue valuation shifts to Comps and DCF once $1M+ ARR exists. A valuation is fragile if it only "works" under an unrealistic exit assumption (e.g., a $1B+ exit) — most VC-backed exits fall in the $50-200M range, and matching the multiple type to company stage (revenue multiples pre-profit, earnings multiples once profitable) is a common diligence check.
