# VC Due Diligence Framework

## What Due Diligence Is
The structured, investor-directed investigation a VC firm conducts before committing capital, distinct from the pitch stage where the founder controls the narrative — in diligence, investors define the questions, timeline, and scope. Early-stage due diligence differs meaningfully from mature-company diligence: without years of financials, investors are effectively betting on people, potential, and an early, often-unproven product.

## Document Categories (25-Document Framework, Papermark 2026)
A comprehensive investment due diligence checklist spans five categories:
1. **Financial** — historical financials, projections, cap table, burn/runway data (see Finance section documents)
2. **Legal** — corporate structure, IP ownership, material contracts, litigation history
3. **Operational** — team org chart, key-person dependencies, vendor/customer concentration
4. **Commercial** — market sizing, competitive positioning, customer references, pipeline/retention data
5. **Technical** — architecture review, security posture, technical debt assessment

## Investor Time Investment
VC deal teams reportedly spend at least 20 hours per deal on due diligence on average (Qubit Capital, 2025) — a nontrivial time cost that is itself a form of screening, since firms cannot apply this depth of scrutiny to every inbound opportunity.

## Rising Focus Areas for 2026
- **Cybersecurity audits**: an estimated 60% of VCs will require a cybersecurity audit as part of due diligence by 2026, reflecting both AI-data-handling exposure and broader enterprise-buyer security expectations (see the B2B SaaS Cybersecurity Risks document for the underlying risk landscape).
- **AI-powered diligence tooling**: VC firms are increasingly using AI/ML-assisted automation to streamline the traditionally manual diligence review process, which may compress diligence timelines but also means founders should expect more systematic cross-referencing of claims across submitted documents.
- **Financial model scrutiny**: a specific, commonly-cited diligence failure point is that 80% of startup financial models reviewed do not clearly show the "cash gap" between current runway and the next planned raise — proactively surfacing this gap (rather than letting diligence discover it) is treated as a credibility signal.

## Founder Preparation Guidance
Because diligence is investor-directed rather than founder-controlled, the most effective preparation is assembling the 25-document checklist proactively before a raise begins, rather than scrambling to produce documents reactively once a term sheet is on the table — delay in document production is itself read as a risk signal by many investors.
