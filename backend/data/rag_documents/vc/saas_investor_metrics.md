# SaaS Investor Metrics: Rule of 40 and Beyond

## The Rule of 40
Rule of 40 = Revenue Growth Rate (%) + Profit Margin (%, typically EBITDA or FCF margin). A company growing 30% with a 15% EBITDA margin scores 45 — considered healthy. It is the single most-cited health metric in SaaS investor conversations because it captures the growth/profitability tradeoff in one number.

## Current Benchmark Reality (2026)
- Median Rule of 40 score for private B2B SaaS: 25% in 2025 — the highest in three years, a 10-point improvement over 2024, but still well below the "40" threshold implied by the metric's name.
- Only 9% of private SaaS companies under $30M ARR clear the 40% threshold; the rate rises to 22-26% at higher ARR bands.
- Among public SaaS companies, the share clearing Rule of 40 fell from ~48% in 2021 to roughly 20-25% by 2024-2026 (strict definition) as rate hikes compressed multiples — the "40" bar has become materially harder to clear across the whole public SaaS universe, not just for any individual company.
- Companies clearing Rule of 40 on a free-cash-flow basis trade at a median 4.8x EV/Revenue vs. 2.7x for those that fail the threshold — a 74% valuation premium tied directly to this single metric.
- Every 10-point improvement in Rule of 40 score correlates with roughly a 1.1x increase in EV/Revenue multiple as of Q4 2025 (up from 0.8x in early 2025) — the market is increasingly rewarding capital efficiency at the margin.

## The Emerging "Rule of X" Alternative
Bessemer Venture Partners has introduced the "Rule of X," which weights revenue growth roughly 2-3x more heavily than free-cash-flow margin, and achieves ~62% R-squared in predicting valuation multiples versus ~50% R-squared for the traditional Rule of 40 — suggesting growth rate remains a somewhat stronger single predictor of multiple than profitability, even as the market has grown more efficiency-conscious.

## Additional Investor Metrics Referenced Alongside Rule of 40
- Magic Number (Net New ARR ÷ Previous Quarter S&M spend): 0.7 considered good, 1.0+ considered great (especially for AI SaaS)
- CAC Payback Period, LTV:CAC, and NRR (see dedicated Finance documents) — Rule of 40 is typically presented alongside these rather than as a standalone metric in institutional investor conversations.

## Practical Framing
Given the median private company scores only 25 (not 40), presenting a Rule of 40 score meaningfully above 25 — even if below the "40" nominal threshold — is a legitimately competitive, above-median position, provided it is framed against the correct 2025-2026 benchmark rather than the metric's nominal name.
