# Competitor Profile: Apollo.io

## Product
AI-powered go-to-market (GTM) platform combining a large B2B contact database with engagement automation and, increasingly, a lightweight CRM. Positioned as an all-in-one alternative to stitching together separate data (ZoomInfo), engagement (Outreach/Salesloft), and CRM (HubSpot) tools.

## Target Customer
Originally SMB/startup sales teams via self-serve, product-led plans ($50-100/month); has since pushed deliberately upmarket, with enterprise accounts reportedly growing 400% in a recent year while retaining a large self-serve SMB base.

## Founding & Near-Death Turnaround
Founded 2015 in San Francisco as ZenProspect by Tim Zheng, Roy Chung, and Ray Li; went through Y Combinator's Winter 2016 batch; rebranded to Apollo.io in 2018. Grew to ~$5M ARR but by 2018-2020 was running an unsustainable sales-led motion on ~$10K contracts — spending a dollar to acquire only eighty cents of revenue. Compounded by a data breach and revenue decline, the company had roughly six months of runway left by April 2020. It then executed a full pivot from sales-led to product-led growth, rebuilding the product, GTM motion, and leadership team (see the dedicated case study document for detail).

## Recent Financials
$150M ARR as of May 2025 (up from $134M at year-end 2024, 40% YoY growth); $96M ARR in 2023; $48M in 2022; $24M in 2021; $8M in 2020; $5M in 2019 — a trajectory from near-death to one of the fastest-growing companies in SaaS. Raised $100M Series D in August 2023 led by Bain Capital Ventures at a $1.6B valuation (a ~16.7x revenue multiple on 2023 ARR), becoming the first sales-tech unicorn minted in 2023. Total funding to date: $251.3M. Nearly 100,000 paying customers as of the most recent public figures.

## Strengths
Product-led growth motion delivers materially lower CAC than sales-led incumbents; vertically integrated data + engagement + (emerging) CRM reduces the multi-vendor stack buyers otherwise assemble; fast, self-serve time-to-value vs. Outreach/Salesloft's longer enterprise implementation cycles; aggressive AI feature shipping cadence (Scores & Signals, Smart Meeting Suite, Workflows).

## Weaknesses
Contact/company data — Apollo's original moat — is increasingly commoditized by AI-powered scraping, with GTM teams shifting budget toward signal-based tools like Clay ($30M ARR in 2024, growing 500% YoY); competing simultaneously against both data incumbents (ZoomInfo) and all-in-one incumbents (HubSpot) as it pushes into CRM territory increases the number of well-funded fronts it must defend.
