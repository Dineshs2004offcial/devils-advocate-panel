# Case Study: A SaaS Pivot Post-Mortem — Zenfetch's Move Into Sales Tech

## The Original Idea
The founder (who had left Stripe to start the company, with co-founder Akash) originally set out to build a product helping people "unlock the 90% of information they read and forget about" — a consumer-oriented knowledge-retention tool, not a B2B sales product.

## The Premature Pivot
The day before leaving Stripe, the founder asked CEO Patrick Collison for advice; Collison said, "I see more people give up too late rather than give up too early." The founder later concluded he over-indexed on this advice: within two weeks of starting their YC batch, the founders pivoted away from their original consumer idea, having mistakenly concluded there wasn't a viable B2B use case for it and feeling pressure to find a clearer path to B2B revenue.

## The Pivot Destination: Sales Tech
Rather than building around a problem they deeply understood, the founders searched for a B2B problem area they believed would generate revenue, and — after speaking with hundreds of GTM leaders under YC demo-day time pressure — landed on a specific thesis: sales reps with deep product knowledge close more deals than those without, built as "Zenfetch," a tool that gave reps in-meeting access to internal company documentation.

## Why the Pivot Failed
Despite the founder still believing the underlying thesis is sound, three specific execution failures are cited:
1. **High-touch product friction**: the product required deep access to internal company documentation and a bot presence in every sales meeting — a heavy integration/trust burden for a young, unproven vendor to ask of enterprise sales teams.
2. **Wrong-priority market timing**: GTM leaders in the target market were, at that time, far more focused on top-of-funnel lead generation and churn prevention than on mid-funnel conversion — the exact problem Zenfetch was solving was not the market's top priority.
3. **Absence of founder-market fit**: the founders "weren't building for ourselves" and were not even using the tool in their own workflow — they lacked genuine empathy for a B2B sales rep's day-to-day experience, having pivoted into the space for perceived revenue potential rather than lived pain.

## Lesson for a Fundraising Narrative
This case is a direct, instructive contrast to the College Conductor case study (where the founder built for a close contact's genuine, lived pain point) — Zenfetch illustrates the risk of pivoting into a B2B category primarily because it looks more fundable, without either deep domain empathy or validated market timing, even when the underlying thesis is directionally reasonable.

Sources: Engineering Serendipity Substack, "Sales Tech: A Post-Mortem" (first-person founder account).
