# SaaS Burn Rate, Runway & Growth Benchmarks (2026)

## Monthly Burn by Stage (CB Insights + Carta 2025, via StealthAgents synthesis)
| Stage | Company profile | Median monthly gross burn |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed | 1-4 people, pre-revenue | $28,000 |
| Seed | 5-12 people, early revenue | $58,000 |
| Series A | 15-40 people, $500K-$3M ARR | $340,000 |
| Series B | 40-120 people, $3M-$15M ARR | $980,000 |
| Series C | 100-300 people, $15M-$60M ARR | $2.8M |
Separate PitchBook-sourced figures cite $50K-$150K median monthly burn at seed rising to $500K-$2M/month at Series B — directionally consistent, with some variance by source.

## Burn Multiple (Net Burn ÷ Net New ARR) — Bessemer 2025 Benchmark
| Burn multiple | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Under 1.0x | Outstanding |
| 1.0x-1.5x | Good |
| 1.5x-2.0x | Acceptable |
| 2.0x-3.0x | High; needs improvement |
| Over 3.0x | Unsustainable |
A burn multiple below 1.5x is now the standard benchmark for capital efficiency at Series A/B (Bessemer State of the Cloud 2025); AI-native companies are reportedly achieving sub-1.0x burn multiples that traditional SaaS companies find nearly impossible to match.

## Runway Benchmarks
18 months runway is the standard investor expectation (Y Combinator); some 2026 commentary pushes this to 24 months as "the new 18," with some VCs favoring 36 months. Despite this, the median funded startup holds only 12-15 months of runway at any given time (Carta 2025), and First Round Capital's 2024 State of Startups survey found 61% of founders feel they have less than 18 months of runway, with 29% reporting less than 12 months — a persistent gap between the runway founders want (18-24 months, cited by 67% as their target) and what they actually carry.

## Failure Attribution
29-38% of startup failures cite running out of cash or failing to raise new capital as the primary cause (CB Insights) — the single largest cited failure driver, ahead of "no market need" in some analyses (see the Failed SaaS Startup case study for a "no market need" example).

## Fundraising Timing Guidance
Y Combinator and multiple 2026 sources converge on: begin raising with 9-12 months of runway remaining; never let runway drop below 6 months during an active raise. Median time between funding rounds has stretched to nearly 700 days (Carta), meaning founders should start fundraising earlier than intuition suggests.

Sources: StealthAgents "Startup Burn Rate Benchmarks (2026)" and "Startup Runway Statistics 2026" (citing PitchBook Q1 2026 Venture Monitor, Bessemer State of the Cloud 2025, CB Insights, Carta, First Round Capital State of Startups 2024); Warp.co "Burn Rate Startup Guide."
